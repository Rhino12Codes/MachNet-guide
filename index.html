Robotic cases are paid the same way as manual cases.

One case rate per procedure. The rate is set by the procedure performed, not by the tools used to perform it.

Medicare and commercial payers pay a hospital one case rate for each coronary procedure. It covers the stents, balloons, wires, imaging and the robotic cassette used in the case. No additional code is required to bill a robotic case, and no additional code pays for one. The question for hospital leadership is how much of each case payment the consumables use, and how much remains.

What status J1 means for the cassette

J1 marks a comprehensive APC: Medicare makes a single payment for the primary service and packages every adjunctive item on the claim into it. A device without its own pass-through code is reported with revenue code 0272 or 0278 and its charges roll into that payment. Adding the cassette does not add a claim line that pays and does not put the payment at risk. It changes the cost of the case, not the payment.

What commercial payers pay for the same case

Commercial contracts pay more for the same case

Commercial contracts are negotiated on the same architecture: one case rate per procedure, packaged. For a single-vessel stent PCI, posted hospital contracts place the national carriers at 1.33 to 1.54 times the Medicare rate (Humana 1.33, Aetna 1.44, UnitedHealthcare and Anthem 1.51, Cigna 1.54). The gap is widest on diagnostic catheterization.6

Precedent

Coronary intravascular lithotripsy entered the market as a packaged adjunct disposable. Its stent cases now group to their own MS-DRGs (323 to 325), paying $10,500 to $12,700 more than the standard PCI DRGs. The payment system recognizes devices with an evidence case; it starts them inside the existing case rate.3
